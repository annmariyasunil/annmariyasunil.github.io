What I work on

  • Attacking congested networks where users route selfishly

    An interdictor slows down roads on a budget; travelers settle into a new equilibrium. I compute the attack that hurts most, also when demand is uncertain, with two exact algorithms.

    Main PhD project, with Manish Bansal

  • Risk-averse scheduling for a data-center microgrid

    A two-stage stochastic MILP that limits energy shortfalls in the worst scenarios using CVaR, solved by Benders decomposition.

    With VT Electrical & Computer Engineering

  • Pricing a product that is about to be withdrawn

    A Stackelberg game between a manufacturer and two competing retailers when one of them may lose demand. Closed-form equilibria and when a retailer exits.

    Under review, Production Planning & Control

  • Making a tidal simulation actually run

    Rebuilt my master's thesis solver for shallow-water equations, taking multi-cycle simulations from intractable to seconds.

    Manuscript in preparation

Background

I'm a first-year PhD student in Industrial & Systems Engineering at Virginia Tech, advised by Manish Bansal. I came to operations research from mathematics: an M.Sc. from NIT Calicut, where I graduated first in my class with the Institute Gold Medal, and a B.Sc. (Honours) from St. Stephen's College, Delhi.

My tools are mixed-integer and bilevel programming, stochastic and distributionally robust optimization, and decomposition methods, implemented in Python and Gurobi and run at scale on a computing cluster.
